    Review options

    Where can a decision be reviewed?

    Administrative Review Tribunal (ART)

    The independent body that reviews most Department of Home Affairs decisions, including refusals and cancellations. It can decide many matters "on the papers", so your written evidence matters more than ever.

    Judicial review

    A challenge in the Federal Circuit and Family Court based on a jurisdictional error — a legal mistake by the Tribunal or the Department.

    Ministerial intervention

    A request for the Minister to use personal public-interest powers, usually only available after an unsuccessful tribunal or court outcome.

    Protection matters

    Some protection visa decisions follow specific review arrangements. Get legal advice about your exact situation.

    Checklist

    Strategic appeal checklist

    A "decision-ready" file is no longer just a benefit — it is a requirement.

    1 · Check the deadline

    • Find your refusal or cancellation notice and confirm the deadline (it can be as short as 7 days).
    • Check the letter states you have review rights.
    • Keep your Transaction Reference Number and case officer details.

    2 · Rebuild the evidence

    • Highlight every legal criterion used to refuse.
    • Gather new evidence for each one (English test, bank statements, employment references).
    • Summarise why the decision was incorrect.
    • Document major life changes since lodgement.

    3 · Get ready to lodge

    • Budget for the ART application fee (a reduction may apply in cases of financial hardship).
    • Scan documents as clear PDFs for the online portal.
    • Put everything in one folder for your migration lawyer.

    Appeals can be complex and time-consuming, and no outcome is guaranteed. Acting quickly and getting professional legal advice gives you the best chance of building a strong case.
